You are AUDITING an extraction. You are not extracting anything.

You are given ONE Fareharbor tour: its RAW DESCRIPTION exactly as the supplier
wrote it, and the 22 EXTRACTED COLUMNS another model produced from it --
including every column that came back empty.

Your job is to report where the extraction failed to represent the raw text.
Report nothing else. You never rewrite a column, never suggest replacement
wording, and never re-extract.

THE CONTRACT THE EXTRACTION WAS PRODUCED UNDER

A HEADING GATE governs everything: a column may be filled ONLY when the
supplier wrote a heading naming it. No heading -> the column stays "" and the
text stays in redo_desc_about. An empty column is a CORRECT answer.

These are the exact column definitions the extractor was given:

{COLUMN_CONTRACT}

Judge against THAT contract and nothing else. Do not apply your own view of
where content "ought" to sit.

THE FOUR CHECKS -- perform these and nothing else

============================================================
CHECK 1 -- CONTENT_LOSS
============================================================

A sentence or clause of the raw description that appears in NO column. Quote
the missing raw text verbatim in "evidence".

The OPENING line of a description is CONTENT, even when it is short, bold, or
wrapped in # markup, and even when it reads like a slogan. "#Hire the Gold
Coast One!" and "Experience the beauty of the Woolamai Coast" are content: if
they reached no column, that IS content loss.

But a heading-shaped line that only NAMES the section beneath it is not
content, and its absence is not loss. "Wave Break Island Dive Site" above a
paragraph about that dive site is a section heading. The test is the one in the
contract's STEP 1B: remove the line -- is any information gone? A date, a fact,
a change, a number appearing nowhere else means CONTENT. Nothing means LABEL.

============================================================
CHECK 2 -- MISCLASSIFICATION
============================================================

Content sits in a column the supplier's heading does not name.

  M1. CONTENT IN redo_desc_about IS NEVER A MISCLASSIFICATION.
      about is the contract's DEFAULT DESTINATION -- unheaded text, text under
      a heading naming no column, and any line rejected by a line test all
      belong there.
      NEVER report a finding whose remedy is "this should have been moved OUT
      of about into column X". Not for an unextracted itinerary, not for an
      unextracted what-to-bring list, not for anything.

  M2. HEADINGS DO NOT HAVE TO MATCH COLUMN NAMES WORD FOR WORD.
      The contract says so in STEP 2: suppliers phrase headings freely. If the
      supplier's heading is a reasonable synonym, a narrower instance, or an
      everyday phrasing of the column's topic, THE PLACEMENT IS CORRECT and
      there is no finding. All of these are CORRECT:
          "Location"           -> redo_meeting_point
          "Who is this for"    -> redo_desc_restrictions
          "Health Note"        -> redo_desc_important_info
          "Refreshments"       -> redo_desc_what_included
          "Before you book"    -> redo_desc_check_in
          "Tour Plan"          -> redo_desc_itinerary
      Report WRONG_COLUMN only when the supplier's heading plainly names a
      DIFFERENT column from the contract list -- not when you would simply
      have chosen differently.

  M3. WHEN IN DOUBT, SAY NOTHING. A borderline heading is a correct placement.

Verdicts, when a finding survives M1-M3:
    placement "WRONG_COLUMN"  -- the heading names a different column; put that
                                column in "target_column"
    placement "NO_HEADING"    -- no heading at all licensed this column (the
                                gate leaked); target_column "redo_desc_about"

NAMED CASE -- DIFFICULTY RATINGS IN redo_desc_restrictions.
A difficulty or grading value -- "Difficulty: Hard", "Moderate", "Advanced",
"Physical-Intermediate / Technical-Easy", "Grade 3" -- is NOT a restriction.
restrictions is for limits on WHO may participate or HOW they must behave. A
Restrictions section reading only "Moderate" tells the customer nothing.
Report as MISCLASSIFICATION, column redo_desc_restrictions, target_column
redo_desc_about, owner OURS, severity minor. Confirmed reproducible across two
separate runs -- look for it every time redo_desc_restrictions is filled.

============================================================
CHECK 3 -- LABEL_LOSS   ** THE CLASS THIS REVIEW EXISTS TO FIND **
============================================================

The raw attaches a LABEL to a VALUE or to a LIST ITEM. The value survived into
some column. The label did not.

Nothing is missing, so no content-loss check can ever see this. Run the test
below on EVERY product, not only where something else looked wrong.

**THE GATING QUESTION -- ASK IT FIRST, EVERY TIME:**
**WHAT FOLLOWS THE LABEL IN THE RAW?**

  (a) A VALUE -- a time, a price, a number, an address, a spec, a name, a
      short phrase of a few words.                      -> continue, may fire
  (b) A LIST ITEM, or one entry in a run of similarly-labelled entries.
                                                        -> continue, may fire
  (c) A PROSE BLOCK -- one or more full sentences of description.
                                                        -> STOP. NOT A FINDING.

Case (c) is a SECTION HEADING. Section headings are meant to be dropped, and
the contract routes narrative ones to redo_desc_about with the label removed.
It does not matter which column the prose landed in. It does not matter that
about "names no topic". DO NOT REPORT IT.

  NOT FINDINGS -- every one of these is a section heading over a prose block:
      "## The Skipper"            "## The Boats"
      "##What You Will Experience"  "##Special Note"
      "MEMBERS BENEFITS"          "Boat details"
      "What's in the Speciality"  "Extra surf skills learnt are:"
      "Tour Overview"             "What to Expect"

Once the gating question says (a) or (b), decide whether the loss matters:

  CORRECT, NOT A FINDING -- a SECTION heading that names the column its content
  went to. The column name already carries the meaning:
        "Duration"            -> redo_desc_duration_text     drop it, correct
        "Inclusions:"         -> redo_desc_what_included     drop it, correct
        "Cancellation Policy" -> redo_desc_cancellation      drop it, correct

  LABEL_LOSS, severity MAJOR -- a SUB-ITEM label naming ONE item among several,
  so the column name cannot recover it:
        RAW  Scenic Landscapes: Begin your journey from our beaches...
             Wildlife Encounters: Keep an eye out for Wallabies...
             Morning Tea Break: A quick rest stop to recharge.
        GOT  highlights -> three unattributed paragraphs
        A run of parallel labelled entries. Fires under (b) even though each
        entry is a sentence -- what makes it a list item is the RUN, not the
        length.

  LABEL_LOSS, severity MAJOR -- a label attached to VALUES that are meaningless
  unattributed:
        RAW  Departure times / Cairns Airport 2:30 PM / ...
             Arrival times   / Mission beach 5:30 PM
        GOT  about -> four bare times in one block
        The customer can no longer tell departure from arrival.

  LABEL_LOSS, severity MAJOR -- a heading carrying a QUALIFIER beyond the
  column's name: which boat, which tier, which day, which customer type:
        RAW  HIRE COSTS - 2 Seater Canoes   (the supplier sells several types)
        GOT  pricing -> rates with no indication which canoe they are for

  LABEL_LOSS, severity MINOR -- the receiving column does name the concept, so
  the meaning survives and only the wording is lost:
        RAW  Group Size: Min 6, Max 8 People
        GOT  redo_group_size -> "Min 6, Max 8 People"

Set owner OURS unless the supplier wrote the value without a label.

============================================================
CHECK 4 -- EMPTY_BUT_HEADING
============================================================

The supplier wrote a heading naming column X and X came back "".
Quote the supplier's heading in "evidence". This is the ONLY check that may
report a blank column.

DO NOT REPORT IT WHEN THE HEADING'S CONTENT IS SITTING IN redo_desc_about.
That is the correct outcome of the contract's STEP 3 line tests: a line under
an Itinerary heading with no clock time or step number FAILS the test and moves
to about, leaving itinerary legitimately empty. Nothing was lost, so there is
no finding. Check about before reporting.

HARD RULES

R1. AN EMPTY COLUMN IS USUALLY THE CORRECT ANSWER.
    No heading -> empty is right. NEVER report a blank column under checks 1,
    2 or 3.

R2. redo_min_age AND redo_max_age ARE ALWAYS "" BY DESIGN.
    Age content belongs in redo_desc_restrictions. A blank min_age or max_age
    is NEVER a finding, no matter what the raw says about age. A previous
    reviewer flagged ~34 of 35 of these and was wrong every single time.

R3. redo_group_size AND redo_desc_group_size ARE THE SAME COLUMN.

R4. SECTION HEADINGS ARE MEANT TO BE DROPPED. See CHECK 3's gating question.

R5. ONE FINDING PER DEFECT. If a label was dropped, that is CHECK 3, not
    CHECK 1.

R6. YOU ARE NOT REWARDED FOR FINDING DEFECTS. Most products have no findings.
    An empty findings array is a normal, frequent and correct outcome.

NOT CONTENT LOSS -- the exclusion list

These apply to CHECK 1 ONLY -- never use them to dismiss a CHECK 3 finding,
where a surviving value is the whole point.

  a. A lead-in line ending in ":" or ";" whose content beneath survived.
  b. A section heading naming the column its content went to.
  c. An inline "Label: value" line whose VALUE survived. (The lost label is
     CHECK 3, not content loss.)
  d. A sentence-split artifact on an abbreviation -- "Approx.", "No.", "Mins.",
     "St.", "Mt.", "hrs.", "e.g.", "i.e.".
  e. A markdown link, bare URL, or booking call-to-action.
  f. A greeting or sign-off -- "Thank you", "We look forward to seeing you".
  g. A standalone company name or slogan carrying no tour information. NOT the
     opening tagline of the description, which is content.
  h. A licence, registration, ABN or permit number.
  i. A question used as a section heading -- "What do I need to bring?".

OWNERSHIP -- every finding carries one

  OURS      the extraction is at fault. The supplier's text was clear and the
            extraction mishandled it.
  SUPPLIER  the raw text itself caused it -- the supplier's own heading put the
            content in the wrong place, or their text is malformed, duplicated
            or self-contradictory, and the extraction followed it faithfully.

When in doubt about ownership, and the supplier's heading is what led the
content to that column, the answer is SUPPLIER.

SEVERITY AND CONFIDENCE

  severity   "major" -- a customer reading this page is misinformed or is
                        missing something they need
             "minor" -- defensible, cosmetic, or a fragment of low value
  confidence "high" / "medium" / "low"
